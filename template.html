How to read this

  • Source. arXiv's bulk OAI-PMH feed (set math, arXiv metadata), harvested on . A paper is dated by its first version's submission date. Days after are not yet fully announced and are excluded; incomplete weeks, months, quarters and years are dropped rather than shown as a false dip.
  • Primary vs any. "Primary subject" counts each paper once, under its first category (only papers whose primary category is a math subject). "Any listed subject" also counts cross-lists, so a paper listed under math.AG and math.AT appears in both. Aliases are merged: math.MP = math-ph, math.IT = cs.IT, math.ST = stat.TH.
  • Index. Each series divided by its own 2019 average for the chosen granularity, so subjects of very different sizes can be compared on one axis.
  • Daily counts dip every weekend because submissions are dated by the day they arrive, not the day they are announced. Smoothing applies a centered 7-day, 5-week or 3-month average.
  • AI releases are the public launch dates of frontier models (from company announcements and Wikipedia). "Major" is a hand-picked subset; "All" adds the point releases. The optional milestones layer adds mathematics-specific AI results and the start of the COVID-19 pandemic, which caused its own 2020 submission surge.
  • Caution. arXiv math volume has grown steadily for two decades; a rise after a release date is not, by itself, evidence that the release caused it.

Release dates: OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, xAI and DeepSeek announcements; Wikipedia articles on GPT-5, GPT-5.2, GPT-5.5, GPT-6, Claude and Gemini (retrieved 20 Sep 2026).
